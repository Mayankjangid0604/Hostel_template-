The Story

Built by a librarian's family, for students who read late.

SaraswatiPerch began as a single rented floor above a bookshop, opened by a family who believed that a good education needs a good place to rest. Fifteen years on, it has grown into a full residence — but the idea hasn't changed: a calm perch, close to campus, where studying comes first.

Every room is arranged around the same principle: light in the morning, quiet in the evening, and a desk that is never an afterthought. We are proud that so many of our residents have gone from these rooms into their board exams, their entrance tests, and their first jobs.

A Day at SaraswatiPerch

The Rhythm of the House

A predictable day keeps the mind free for what matters. Here is what an ordinary weekday looks like.

6:30 — 8:30 AM

Morning

Doors open early for those headed to class or the library. A simple breakfast is laid out in the common mess, and the courtyard is quiet enough for a last read-through of notes.

1:00 — 2:00 PM

Midday

Lunch is served on the hour, followed by a lull most residents use for a short nap or a walk to the corner tea stall before afternoon lectures resume.

7:00 — 10:30 PM

Evening

Dinner, then the house settles into its longest and quietest stretch — desk lamps on, corridors hushed, the reading room open until lights-out at 11.
